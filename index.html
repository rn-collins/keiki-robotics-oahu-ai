Meet Hawaiʻi's Championship Robotics Teams

Getting your keiki into competitive robotics.

A plain, friendly first-steps guide for families. You don't have to leave these islands to build something the world competes with.

What “competitive robotics” means

Kids design, build, and program a robot, then take it to tournaments to complete challenges and go head-to-head with other teams. It teaches the real stuff: problem-solving, teamwork, coding, and showing up under pressure. And it's fun. The biggest program in Hawaiʻi schools is VEX, which runs by age:

VEX IQ (VIQRC)Elementary and middle school. Snap-together plastic parts, no tools or soldering.1,5
VEX V5 (V5RC)Middle and high school. Metal parts and more advanced building and programming.2,6

Each year brings a new game, a build season, and local tournaments that feed toward the VEX Robotics World Championship.4 In 2025, more than 2,400 teams from over 60 countries competed there.3

How to start

Tap each step as you go.

  • ✓Find a team. Ask your keiki's school if it has a VEX team.
  • ✓No team nearby? Try a club. Island Robotics in Waipahu runs VEX across elementary, middle, and high school.7
  • ✓Start with VEX IQ if they're young. No soldering, no scary tools, lots of early wins.1,5
  • ✓Watch a tournament first. Hawaiʻi libraries and schools host robotics events and demos.10
  • ✓Let them build badly at first. Every championship team started with a robot that didn't work.

What it costs (roughly)

Costs vary a lot by team and level. A school team may fold everything into a small program fee. A club charges dues. A VEX IQ kit is the low end and V5 is pricier. Cost is rarely the real barrier: Island Robotics runs its programs at a nominal cost, and Friends of Island Robotics provides equipment and financial support so students who wouldn't otherwise have access can take part.7,8 Ask directly.

What families often get wrong

Tap each one to flip it.

“My kid has to be a coding genius first.”Tap
No. Most kids learn to code because of robotics, not before it.
“We need our own expensive robot.”Tap
No. Teams share kits and space. That's the norm.7
“It's only for high schoolers.”Tap
No. VEX IQ starts in elementary school.1
“You have to win to get anything out of it.”Tap
No. The skills and the friendships come from the build, not the trophy.

A little local inspiration

Coach Jared Haiola of Haleʻiwa Elementary, a 2023 Hawaiʻi State Teacher of the Year finalist,9 coaches the school's “Cane Fires” VEX IQ team and has taken students to the VEX IQ World Championship.10 Oʻahu A.I. credits him with guiding dozens of Haleʻiwa students to Worlds over the past decade.11 Everyone starts with a robot that doesn't work yet. Yours can too.
